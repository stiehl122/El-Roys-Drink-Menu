# Leroy's Lounge Vibe

## Core Read

Leroy's Lounge should feel like a 1970s basement dive bar that has aged into
its own mythology.

This is not a polished cocktail lounge, a hotel bar, or a clean editorial
hospitality brand. It should feel low-slung, warm, analog, and a little worn
in. The room should feel lived in.

## Emotional Target

- Basement bar, not rooftop bar
- American bar-food grit, not precious small plates
- Familiar, dim, smoky, and trustworthy
- Slightly rough edges, but intentional
- More "your favorite hidden late-night spot" than "premium mixology concept"

## Visual Vocabulary

- Amber light
- Dark wood paneling
- Brass that has picked up age
- Worn leather booths
- Tobacco browns
- Nicotine cream
- Faded burgundy
- Beer-sign glow
- Paper menu mats
- Scuffed bar tops
- Analog signage

## Materials And Texture

- Walnut and dark-stained wood
- Aged brass or bronze
- Leather, vinyl, and cracked upholstery energy
- Smoked glass
- Paper flyers and taped-up notices
- Light grain, halation, and wear

Nothing should feel sterile or freshly manufactured.

## Color Direction

Use warm, dirty, grounded tones first:

- amber
- tobacco
- walnut
- brass
- cream with age in it
- deep oxblood or faded burgundy as a minor accent

Avoid bright candy color and avoid clean white as a dominant surface.

## Typography Direction

- Bold bar-sign confidence
- Condensed display type can work well
- Hand-painted, flyer, or analog sign influence is welcome
- Serif can be used, but it should feel grounded and tactile, not literary or luxury-editorial

Avoid:

- airy fashion serif systems
- startup-clean sans systems
- wine-bar minimalism

## Layout And Motion

- Heavy, grounded composition
- Confident blocks over delicate airy grids
- Motion should feel slow, weighted, and analog
- Reveal and hover effects should feel like glow, slide, flicker, or print wear, not slick SaaS polish

## Food And Menu Framing

Leroy's is the American-bites side of the house.

The food framing should suggest:

- burgers
- fries
- late-night plates
- bar snacks with real heft

## What To Avoid

- generic modern restaurant minimalism
- boutique speakeasy tropes
- polished hotel-lobby luxury
- bright brunch energy
- "clean editorial magazine" as the primary mood
