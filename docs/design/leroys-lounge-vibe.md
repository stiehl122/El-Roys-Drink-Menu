# Leroy's Lounge Vibe

## Core Read

Leroy's Lounge should feel like a literal hometown basement dive/sports bar
with 1970s rec-room bones.

This is not a polished cocktail lounge, a hotel bar, or a clean editorial
hospitality brand. It is also not a cinematic speakeasy or invented dive-bar
myth. It should feel low-slung, warm, analog, local, and a little crowded in
the way a busy downstairs bar gets crowded.

The most accurate read is a newer Fenton bar deliberately built to feel like it
has always been there: sports on TV, classic rock, friendly bartenders, posted
specials, cold domestic beer, pool-table presence, Keno/pull-tab energy, and a
short old-school food menu.

## Emotional Target

- Basement bar, not rooftop bar
- Hometown sports dive, not cocktail den
- American bar-food directness, not precious small plates
- Familiar, dim, loud enough, and trustworthy
- Slightly rough edges, but intentional
- More "game's on downstairs" than "premium mixology concept"
- New bar made to feel old, not old bar turned into a theme

## Visual Vocabulary

- Amber light
- Dark wood paneling
- Black and brown menu boards
- Brown/black booth or upholstery cues
- Tobacco browns
- Nicotine cream
- Beer-sign glow
- Faded beer red as a small accent
- Hamm's/PBR blue as an occasional note
- TV blue as reflected light only
- Paper specials slips
- Posted wall menu
- Scuffed bar tops
- Pool table
- Jukebox
- Keno and pull-tab energy
- Analog signage and taped notices

## Materials And Texture

- Walnut and dark-stained wood
- Aged brass, bronze, or yellowed trim
- Brown and black leather/vinyl energy
- Worn blackboard or menu-board surfaces
- Laminated specials and handled paper
- Paper flyers and taped-up notices
- Light grain, halation, board scratches, and weekend wear

Nothing should feel sterile or freshly manufactured, but nothing should feel
dirty in a food-safety way. The right wear is handled, local, and physical.

## Color Direction

Use warm, dirty, grounded tones first:

- amber
- tobacco
- walnut
- blackboard brown-black
- aged brass or yellowed trim
- cream with age in it
- faded beer red as a minor accent
- Hamm's/PBR blue as a minor reflected or label accent

Avoid bright candy color, clean white as a dominant surface, and red as a
primary material or booth/upholstery color.

## Typography Direction

- Bold bar-sign confidence
- Condensed display type can work well
- Posted-menu, price-board, and specials-board influence is welcome
- Blocky sign lettering is better than elegant display typography
- Small stamped or taped-note labels can carry utility text

Avoid:

- airy fashion serif systems
- startup-clean sans systems
- wine-bar minimalism
- polished cocktail-menu styling
- novelty fake-vintage fonts

## Layout And Motion

- Heavy, grounded composition
- Denser wall-menu structure over delicate airy grids
- Content can feel posted, taped, stamped, stacked, and slightly crowded
- Motion should feel modest, physical, and analog
- Reveal and hover effects should feel like glow, small lift, sign flicker, or posted information appearing, not slick SaaS polish
- Keep phone scanning fast even when the visual language gets louder

## Food And Menu Framing

Leroy's is the American-bites side of the house.

The food framing should suggest:

- burgers
- chili
- wings
- fries
- fried pickles
- pizza
- fried bologna specials
- cold domestic beer
- bar snacks with real heft

Use plainspoken, local food language. Favor "burgers," "chili," "fried
pickles," "posted specials," "pool," "game's on," "cold beer," "downstairs,"
and "open late" over atmospheric manifesto copy.

## What To Avoid

- generic modern restaurant minimalism
- boutique speakeasy tropes
- cocktail-den romance
- polished hotel-lobby luxury
- bright brunch energy
- "clean editorial magazine" as the primary mood
- generic sports-brand graphics
- fake grime or haunted-house distress
- nostalgic theme-restaurant styling
